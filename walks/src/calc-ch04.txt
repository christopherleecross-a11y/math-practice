a(t) = v′(t) = 6t − 18 || Acceleration is the derivative of velocity.

a(1) = 6 − 18 = −12 m/s² || Acceleration is negative 12 meters per second squared.

@@ calc-4-2 | Example 1
L(x) = f(a) + f′(a)(x − a) || Example 1. The linear approximation is the tangent line at a.
f(4) = 2;  f′(x) = 1/(2√x) → f′(4) = 1/4 || f of 4 is 2. The derivative of root x is 1 over 2 root x, which is one fourth at 4.
L(x) = 2 + (1/4)(x − 4) = (1/4)x + 1 || Simplify: one fourth x plus 1.
√4.1 ≈ L(4.1) = 1.025 + 1 = 2.025 || Use the line at 4.1: about 2.025.

@@ calc-4-2 | Example 2

√26 ≈ 5 + (1/10)(26 − 25) = 5.1 || Move 1 unit right: 5 plus one tenth is 5.1.

@@ calc-4-2 | Example 3

@@ calc-4-2 | Example 4

dr/r = ±1% → dV/V ≈ ±3% || A 1 percent error in the radius gives about a 3 percent error in volume.

@@ calc-4-3 | Example 3

@@ calc-4-3 | Example 4

→ 3(1)/2 = 3/2 || At 0, cosine is 1: 3 halves.
