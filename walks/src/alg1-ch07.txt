degree = sum of the variables' exponents || Example 1. The degree of a monomial is the sum of the exponents of its variables. The number in front doesn't count.
a.  (1/2)x³: 3 || Part a. The only variable is x, to the third: degree 3.
b.  −3x¹y⁴: 1 + 4 = 5 || Part b. x has an invisible exponent of 1, and y has 4. 1 plus 4 is 5.
c.  8 = 8x⁰: 0 || Part c. A plain nonzero number has no variable, so its degree is 0.
d.  4x²y¹z³: 2 + 1 + 3 = 6 || Part d. Add all the exponents: 2 plus 1 plus 3 is 6.
