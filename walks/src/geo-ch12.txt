@@ geo-12-1 | Example 1
Polyhedron: every face is a polygon || Example 1. A polyhedron is a solid whose faces are all polygons: flat, with straight sides.
a.  Yes: a pentagonal prism || Part a. All the faces are polygons. Two parallel pentagon bases joined by rectangles make a pentagonal prism.
b.  No: a cone has a curved surface || Part b. A cone has a curved surface and a circular base, which are not polygons. So it is not a polyhedron.
c.  Yes: a square pyramid || Part c. One square base and triangles meeting at a point: all polygons. It is a square pyramid.

@@ geo-12-1 | Example 2
Euler's Theorem: F + V = E + 2 || Example 2. Euler's Theorem says that for any polyhedron, faces plus vertices equals edges plus 2.
12 + V = 30 + 2 || There are 12 faces and 30 edges.
12 + V = 32 || 30 plus 2 is 32.
V = 20 || Subtract 12: there are 20 vertices.

@@ geo-12-1 | Example 3
Faces: 2 hexagons + 6 rectangles = 8 || Example 3. Count the faces: the top and bottom hexagons, plus 6 rectangles around the sides. That is 8 faces.
Vertices: 6 + 6 = 12 || Count the vertices: 6 on the top hexagon and 6 on the bottom. That is 12.
Edges: 6 + 6 + 6 = 18 || Count the edges: 6 around the top, 6 around the bottom, and 6 going up the sides. That is 18.
F + V = 8 + 12 = 20,  E + 2 = 18 + 2 = 20 ✓ || Check Euler's Theorem: faces plus vertices is 20, and edges plus 2 is also 20. It holds.

@@ geo-12-1 | Example 4
a.  cube, plane parallel to a face → square || Example 4, part a. A plane parallel to one face of a cube cuts through the four side faces in four equal segments. The cross section is a square, the same as a face.
b.  cylinder, vertical through the centers → rectangle || Part b. Slicing a cylinder straight down through the centers of both bases gives a rectangle. Its width is the diameter and its height is the cylinder's height.
c.  cone, parallel to the base → circle || Part c. Slicing a cone parallel to its circular base gives a circle, smaller than the base.

@@ geo-12-1 | Example 5
The plane cuts the three faces at that corner. || Example 5. The plane cuts off one corner, so it passes through the three faces that meet at that corner.
Three faces → three sides || Each face it crosses gives one side of the cross section. Three faces means three sides.
Answer: a triangle || So the cross section is a triangle. Tilting the plane differently can make other shapes, even a hexagon.

@@ geo-12-2 | Example 1
S = 2B + Ph || Example 1. The surface area of a prism is the two bases plus the lateral area. Lateral area is the perimeter of the base times the height.
B = 6 · 4 = 24 || The base is a 6 by 4 rectangle: its area is 24.
P = 2(6) + 2(4) = 20 || The base's perimeter is 6 plus 4 plus 6 plus 4, which is 20.
S = 2(24) + 20(3) = 48 + 60 || The height is 3. Two bases is 48, and 20 times 3 is 60.
S = 108 || The surface area is 108 square units.

@@ geo-12-2 | Example 2

S = 2B + Ph = 2(6) + 12(10) || The prism's length, its height, is 10.
S = 12 + 120 = 132 || Two bases is 12, and the lateral area is 120. The surface area is 132 square units.

@@ geo-12-2 | Example 3
