normal curve: 34% | 34% between μ and ±σ;  13.5% from σ to 2σ || Example 1. In a normal distribution, 34 percent of the area is between the mean and 1 standard deviation on each side. 13.5 percent is between 1 and 2 standard deviations.
13.5% + 34% + 34% = 81.5% || The shaded part covers one 13.5 percent piece and two 34 percent pieces. Together that's 81.5 percent.

@@ alg2-9-1 | Example 2
a.  below μ − σ: 13.5% + 2.35% + 0.15% || Example 2, part a. Everything below 1 standard deviation under the mean: 13.5 percent, plus 2.35 percent, plus the tiny 0.15 percent at the end.
a.  = 16% → 0.16 || That's 16 percent, or 0.16. Another way: half is below the mean, and 34 percent of it is within 1 standard deviation, so 50 minus 34 is 16.
b.  above μ + 2σ: 2.35% + 0.15% = 2.5% || Part b. Beyond 2 standard deviations above the mean: 2.35 plus 0.15 is 2.5 percent, or 0.025.

@@ alg2-9-1 | Example 3

c.  0.025 · 500 = 12.5 ≈ 13 || Part c. 2.5 percent of 500 students is 12.5, so about 13 students.

@@ alg2-9-5 | Example 3
margin of error ≈ ±1/√n || Example 3. For a random sample of size n, the margin of error is about plus or minus 1 over the square root of n.
1/√400 = 1/20 = 0.05 = ±5% || The square root of 400 is 20, and 1 over 20 is 0.05, or 5 percent.
56% ± 5% → 51% to 61% || So the true percent is likely between 56 minus 5 and 56 plus 5: 51 to 61 percent.
