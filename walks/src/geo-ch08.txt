A ↔ D,  B ↔ E,  C ↔ F || Example 1. The similarity statement A B C similar to D E F tells which vertices match: A with D, B with E, and C with F.
∠A ≅ ∠D,  ∠B ≅ ∠E,  ∠C ≅ ∠F || In similar triangles, corresponding angles are congruent.
DE/AB = 12/8 = 3/2 || Now compare corresponding sides, new triangle over original. D E over A B is 12 over 8, which is 3 halves.
EF/BC = 18/12 = 3/2 || E F over B C is 18 over 12, also 3 halves.
FD/CA = 15/10 = 3/2 || F D over C A is 15 over 10, also 3 halves.
k = 3/2 || All three ratios are equal. The scale factor from A B C to D E F is 3 halves, and the statement of proportionality is D E over A B equals E F over B C equals F D over C A.

@@ geo-8-4 | Example 1
Triangle Proportionality Theorem || Example 1. D E is parallel to B C, so by the Triangle Proportionality Theorem, it divides the two sides proportionally.
AD/DB = AE/EC || So A D over D B equals A E over E C.
6/4 = 9/x || Substitute: 6 over 4 equals 9 over x.
6x = 36 || Cross multiply: 6 x equals 4 times 9, which is 36.
x = 6 || Divide by 6: E C is 6.

@@ geo-8-4 | Example 3
Three Parallel Lines Theorem || Example 3. When three parallel lines cross two transversals, they divide the transversals proportionally.
12/8 = 15/x || So the pieces on one transversal have the same ratio as the matching pieces on the other: 12 over 8 equals 15 over x.
12x = 120 || Cross multiply: 12 x equals 8 times 15, which is 120.
x = 10 || Divide by 12: x is 10.

@@ geo-8-4 | Example 4
