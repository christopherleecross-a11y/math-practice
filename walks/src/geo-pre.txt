@@ geo-pre-6-4-30 | Example: A(−2, 1) and B(4, 9)
A(−2, 1) and B(4, 9) || This example finds three things about one segment: its middle, its length, and its slope.
Middle: M = ((−2 + 4)/2, (1 + 9)/2) || The middle is the midpoint. Average the x coordinates and average the y coordinates.
M = (2/2, 10/2) = (1, 5) || Negative 2 plus 4 is 2, divided by 2 is 1. 1 plus 9 is 10, divided by 2 is 5. The midpoint is 1 comma 5.
run: 4 − (−2) = 6,  rise: 9 − 1 = 8 || Now go from A to B. x goes from negative 2 to 4, which is 6 across. y goes from 1 to 9, which is 8 up.
Length: d = √(6² + 8²) = √100 = 10 || Those are the legs of a right triangle, and the segment is the hypotenuse. 36 plus 64 is 100, so the length is 10.
Slope: rise/run = 8/6 = 4/3 || The slope is rise over run: 8 over 6, which simplifies to 4 thirds.
Same two legs → length and slope || Notice that the same two numbers, 6 across and 8 up, gave us both the length and the slope.
