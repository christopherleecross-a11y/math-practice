@@ calc-8-1 | Example 1
x² = x + 2 → (x − 2)(x + 1) = 0 → x = −1, 2 || Example 1. Find where the curves meet: x squared equals x plus 2 at negative 1 and 2.
top: x + 2,  bottom: x² || Between them, the line is above the parabola. Test x equals 0: 2 is more than 0.
∫₋₁² (x + 2 − x²) dx = [x²/2 + 2x − x³/3]₋₁² || Integrate top minus bottom.
= 10/3 − (−7/6) = 9/2 || At 2: 10 thirds. At negative 1: negative 7 sixths. The difference is 27 sixths, which is 9 halves.

@@ calc-8-1 | Example 2
on [0, π/4]: cos x ≥ sin x || Example 2. From 0 to pi over 4, cosine is on top.
∫₀^(π/4) (cos x − sin x) dx = [sin x + cos x]₀^(π/4) || The antiderivative of cosine minus sine is sine plus cosine.
= (√2/2 + √2/2) − (0 + 1) = √2 − 1 || At pi over 4: root 2. At 0: 1. The area is root 2 minus 1.

@@ calc-8-1 | Example 3
y² = y + 2 → y = −1, 2 || Example 3. These are functions of y, so integrate with respect to y. The curves meet at y equals negative 1 and 2.
right: y + 2,  left: y² || Use right minus left.
∫₋₁² (y + 2 − y²) dy = 9/2 || It's the same integral as Example 1, so the area is 9 halves.

@@ calc-8-1 | Example 4
x³ = x → x = −1, 0, 1 || Example 4. The curves meet at negative 1, 0, and 1. The top curve switches at 0.
[0, 1]: ∫(x − x³) dx = 1/2 − 1/4 = 1/4 || From 0 to 1, x is on top: area one fourth.
[−1, 0]: ∫(x³ − x) dx = 1/4 || From negative 1 to 0, x cubed is on top: also one fourth.
1/4 + 1/4 = 1/2 || The total area is one half.

@@ calc-8-2 | Example 1
disk: V = π∫ R² dx,  R = √x || Example 1. Revolving about the x-axis makes disks with radius root x.
π∫₀⁴ (√x)² dx = π∫₀⁴ x dx || Root x squared is x.
= π[x²/2]₀⁴ = 8π || x squared over 2 at 4 is 8. The volume is 8 pi.

@@ calc-8-2 | Example 2
about the y-axis → integrate in y;  x = √y || Example 2. Revolving about the y-axis, the disks are horizontal. Write the radius in terms of y: x is root y.
π∫₀⁴ (√y)² dy = π∫₀⁴ y dy || The radius squared is y.
= π[y²/2]₀⁴ = 8π || The volume is 8 pi.

@@ calc-8-2 | Example 3
x = x² → x = 0, 1;  outer R = x, inner r = x² || Example 3. The curves meet at 0 and 1. On that interval, y equals x is farther from the axis, so it's the outer radius.
washer: π∫₀¹ (x² − x⁴) dx || Washers: pi times outer squared minus inner squared.
= π(1/3 − 1/5) = 2π/15 || One third minus one fifth is 2 fifteenths.

@@ calc-8-2 | Example 4
axis y = −1: distances add 1 || Example 4. The axis is y equals negative 1, so every radius is measured from negative 1.
R = x² − (−1) = x² + 1;  r = 0 − (−1) = 1 || The outer radius goes up to the curve: x squared plus 1. The inner radius goes up to the x-axis: 1.
π∫₀¹ [(x² + 1)² − 1²] dx || Set up the washer integral.

@@ calc-8-3 | Example 1
shell: V = 2π∫ (radius)(height) dx || Example 1. Use shells: 2 pi times the radius times the height.
radius x, height x² || About the y-axis, the shell radius is x and the height is x squared.
2π∫₀² x³ dx = 2π[x⁴/4]₀² = 2π(4) = 8π || The volume is 8 pi.

@@ calc-8-3 | Example 2
radius = distance from x to the axis || Example 2. The shell radius is the distance from x to the axis of rotation.
x − (−1) = x + 1 || The axis is x equals negative 1, to the left of the region, so the radius is x plus 1.

@@ calc-8-3 | Example 3
axis x = 3 is to the right → radius 3 − x || Example 3. Now the axis is to the right of the region, so the radius is 3 minus x.
height: 2x − x²,  x from 0 to 2 || The height is the curve. It crosses the x-axis at 0 and 2.
2π∫₀² (3 − x)(2x − x²) dx || Set up the integral.

@@ calc-8-3 | Example 4
disks need x in terms of y: hard to solve || Example 4. Disks would need x as a function of y, which means solving a quadratic. Shells keep everything in x.
2π∫₀³ x(3x − x²) dx = 2π∫₀³ (3x² − x³) dx || Radius x, height 3 x minus x squared.
= 2π[x³ − x⁴/4]₀³ = 2π(27 − 81/4) || Evaluate at 3.
= 2π(27/4) = 27π/2 || 27 minus 20.25 is 6.75, which is 27 fourths. The volume is 27 pi over 2.

@@ calc-8-4 | Example 1
side s = √x → A(x) = s² = x || Example 1. Each square's side is the height of the region, root x. Its area is x.
V = ∫₀⁴ x dx = [x²/2]₀⁴ = 8 || Integrate the area: 8.

@@ calc-8-4 | Example 2
diameter s = √x → A = (1/2)π(s/2)² = πs²/8 || Example 2. Each semicircle has diameter root x, so its radius is half that. Its area is pi s squared over 8.
A(x) = πx/8 || s squared is x.
V = (π/8)∫₀⁴ x dx = (π/8)(8) = π || The volume is pi.

@@ calc-8-4 | Example 3
s = 1 − x²;  equilateral: A = (√3/4)s² || Example 3. Each triangle's side is the height 1 minus x squared. An equilateral triangle has area root 3 over 4 times s squared.
∫₋₁¹ (1 − x²)² dx = ∫(1 − 2x² + x⁴) dx = 16/15 || Expand and integrate from negative 1 to 1: 2 minus 4 thirds plus 2 fifths is 16 fifteenths.
V = (√3/4)(16/15) = 4√3/15 || Multiply: 4 root 3 over 15.

@@ calc-8-4 | Example 4
legs = s (the base) || Example 4. An isosceles right triangle with a leg on the base has both legs equal to s.
A = (1/2) · s · s = (1/2)s² || Its area is one half times leg times leg.

@@ calc-8-5 | Example 1
L = ∫ from a to b of √(1 + (f′(x))²) dx || Example 1. Arc length is the integral of root 1 plus f prime squared.
f′(x) = 3x² → (f′)² = 9x⁴ || The derivative of x cubed is 3 x squared. Squared: 9 x to the fourth.
∫₀² √(1 + 9x⁴) dx || That's the setup.

@@ calc-8-5 | Example 2
f′(x) = x^(1/2) → (f′)² = x || Example 2. The derivative of 2 thirds x to the 3 halves is root x. Squared: x.
∫₀³ √(1 + x) dx = (2/3)[(1 + x)^(3/2)]₀³ || Integrate.
= (2/3)(8 − 1) = 14/3 || 4 to the 3 halves is 8, and 1 to the 3 halves is 1. 2 thirds times 7 is 14 thirds.

@@ calc-8-5 | Example 3
f′(x) = x²/2 − 1/(2x²) || Example 3. Differentiate.
1 + (f′)² = (x²/2 + 1/(2x²))² || When you square f prime and add 1, the middle term flips sign. It becomes a perfect square.
∫₁² (x²/2 + 1/(2x²)) dx = [x³/6 − 1/(2x)]₁² || The square root undoes the square. Integrate.
= 13/12 − (−1/3) = 17/12 || At 2: 8 sixths minus one fourth, 13 twelfths. At 1: negative one third. The length is 17 twelfths.

@@ calc-8-5 | Example 4
f′(x) = 2x → ∫₀² √(1 + 4x²) dx || Example 4. The derivative is 2 x, so the integrand is root 1 plus 4 x squared.
calculator: ≈ 4.647 || Use the calculator's integral feature: about 4.647.

@@ calc-8-6 | Example 1
W = ∫ F(x) dx || Example 1. Work is the integral of force over distance.
∫₅²⁰ (40 − x) dx = [40x − x²/2]₅²⁰ || Integrate.
= 600 − 187.5 = 412.5 J || At 20: 800 minus 200 is 600. At 5: 200 minus 12.5 is 187.5. The work is 412.5 joules.

@@ calc-8-6 | Example 2
Hooke's law: F = kx → 3 = k(0.25) → k = 12 || Example 2. Spring force is k x. 3 newtons stretches it 0.25 meters, so k is 12.
W = ∫₀¹ 12x dx = [6x²]₀¹ = 6 J || Integrate from 0 to 1: 6 joules.

@@ calc-8-6 | Example 3
a slice y ft down weighs 3 dy, lifted y ft || Example 3. A small piece of chain y feet below the bridge weighs 3 d y pounds and must be lifted y feet.
W = ∫₀⁴⁰ 3y dy = [3y²/2]₀⁴⁰ || Add up all the slices.
= 3(1600)/2 = 2400 ft-lb || 3 times 1600 over 2 is 2400 foot pounds.

@@ calc-8-6 | Example 4
slice: volume π(2²) dy, weight 62.4 · 4π dy || Example 4. A thin slice of water is a disk with radius 2. Water weighs 62.4 pounds per cubic foot.
lifted y ft (y = depth below the top) || A slice y feet below the top is lifted y feet.
∫₀⁵ 62.4(4π) y dy = 249.6π(25/2) || Integrate y from 0 to 5: 25 halves.
= 3120π ≈ 9802 ft-lb || 249.6 times 12.5 is 3120. The work is 3120 pi, about 9802 foot pounds.
