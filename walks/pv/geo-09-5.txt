title: Sine of an angle
sin = opposite/hypotenuse || Sine is the opposite side over the hypotenuse. SOH.
from J: opposite = 10, hypotenuse = 26 || Stand at angle J. The side across from it is 10, and the hypotenuse, across from the right angle, is 26.
sin J = 10/26 = 5/13 ≈ 0.3846 || 10 over 26 simplifies to 5 thirteenths, about 0.3846.

title: Cosine of an angle
cos = adjacent/hypotenuse || Cosine is the adjacent side over the hypotenuse. CAH.
from L: adjacent = 8, hypotenuse = 17 || Stand at angle L. The leg next to it is 8, and the hypotenuse is 17.
cos L = 8/17 ≈ 0.4706 || So cosine L is 8 over 17, about 0.4706.

kind: in terms of sine
title: Complementary angles: write cosine as sine
sin A = cos(90° − A) || The sine of an angle equals the cosine of its complement, and the other way around.
cos 69° = sin(90° − 69°) || The complement of 69 is 21.
= sin 21° || So cosine 69 equals sine 21.

title: Find legs with sine and cosine
hypotenuse 10, angle 28° || The hypotenuse is 10 and the angle is 28 degrees.
x is opposite: sin 28° = x/10 → x = 10 sin 28° ≈ 4.7 || x is across from the angle, so use sine: x is 10 sine 28, about 4.7.
y is adjacent: cos 28° = y/10 → y = 10 cos 28° ≈ 8.8 || y is next to the angle, so use cosine: y is 10 cosine 28, about 8.8.
