@@ p10HasInverse 1000
title: The Horizontal Line Test
sweep horizontal lines across the graph || Picture horizontal lines moving up and down across the graph.
some horizontal line crosses more than once → no inverse || Here, a horizontal line hits the wave in more than one place. Two inputs give the same output, so the function isn't one-to-one and has no inverse function.
every horizontal line crosses at most once → inverse exists || If every horizontal line hits the graph at most once, the function is one-to-one and has an inverse. Lines, cubics, and square root curves pass. Parabolas and V shapes fail.

title: Inverse values from a table
f(a) = b ⟺ f⁻¹(b) = a || The inverse switches inputs and outputs. To find f inverse of a number, find that number in the f of x row.
f⁻¹(−5): f(3) = −5 → 3 || Negative 5 is under x equals 3, so f inverse of negative 5 is 3.
f⁻¹(−2) = 2;  f⁻¹(13) = −3 || In the same way, negative 2 is under 2, and 13 is under negative 3.

@@ p10Verify 1000
title: Verify inverse functions
f(g(x)) = √((x² + 1) − 1) = √(x²) = x  (x ≥ 0) || Put g into f. The 1s cancel, and the square root of x squared is x, since x is at least 0.
g(f(x)) = (√(x − 1))² + 1 = x − 1 + 1 = x || Put f into g. Squaring undoes the square root.
both give x → inverses || Both compositions give x, so f and g are inverses. If either one doesn't give x, they aren't.

@@ p10FindInverse 1020

title: Find the inverse of a linear function
y = −5x + 8 → x = −5y + 8 || Write y for f of x, then switch x and y.
5y = 8 − x → y = (8 − x)/5 || Solve for y: add 5 y and subtract x, then divide by 5.
f⁻¹(x) = −(1/5)x + 8/5 || So f inverse is negative one fifth x plus 8 fifths.

@@ p10FindInverse 1001
kind: x³
title: Find the inverse of a cubic
y = x³ − 4 → x = y³ − 4 || Switch x and y.
y³ = x + 4 → y = ∛(x + 4) || Add 4, then take the cube root of both sides.
f⁻¹(x) = ∛(x + 4) || That's the inverse.

@@ p10FindInverse 1003
kind: x ≥
title: Find the inverse of a restricted square
y = (x + 4)², x ≥ −4 → x = (y + 4)² || Switch x and y. The restriction makes f one-to-one, so the inverse exists.
y + 4 = √x  (positive root, since y ≥ −4) || Take the square root. Use the positive root, because the outputs of the inverse are at least negative 4.
f⁻¹(x) = √x − 4 || Subtract 4.

@@ p10FindInverse 1000

title: Find the inverse of a square root function
y = √(x − 1) → x = √(y − 1) || Switch x and y.
x² = y − 1 → y = x² + 1 || Square both sides, then add 1.
f⁻¹(x) = x² + 1, x ≥ 0 || The square root only gives outputs of 0 or more, so the inverse only uses x at least 0.

title: Find the inverse of a rational function
x = (y + 5)/(y − 2) || Switch x and y.
x(y − 2) = y + 5 → xy − 2x = y + 5 || Multiply both sides by y minus 2 and distribute.
