title: What you can conclude from a diagram
You CAN assume: collinear points, intersecting lines, vertical angles, linear pairs || From a diagram you can assume things that come from how lines and points are connected: points on a line are collinear, lines that cross do intersect, and angles that look like vertical angles or a linear pair are.
You CAN'T assume: lengths, congruence, midpoints, right angles (unless marked) || You can't assume anything about size: lengths, congruent segments or angles, midpoints, or right angles. Those need marks in the diagram.
H, J, F are on one line → ∠HJE and ∠EJF are a linear pair || Here H, J, and F lie on one line, so angles H J E and E J F form a linear pair.
m∠HJE + m∠EJF = 180° || A linear pair adds to 180 degrees, so this statement can be concluded.

@@ g23Assume 1000

title: What you can NOT conclude from a diagram
You CAN'T assume sizes unless the diagram marks them. || You can't assume lengths, congruence, midpoints, or right angles unless the diagram marks them.
single ticks: T̅U̅ ≅ U̅L̅;  double ticks: H̅U̅ ≅ U̅Q̅ || The single tick marks show T U is congruent to U L. The double tick marks show H U is congruent to U Q.

@@ g23Error 1000
title: Error analysis: diagrams
“U is the midpoint of H̅Q̅ and T̅L̅.” || To be a midpoint, the diagram has to show the two halves are congruent.
T̅U̅ ≅ U̅L̅ is marked → U is the midpoint of T̅L̅ ✓ || T U and U L have matching tick marks, so U is the midpoint of T L.
nothing marks H̅U̅ ≅ U̅Q̅ || H U and U Q have no tick marks. They might look equal, but you can't assume it.
U is the midpoint of T̅L̅ only. || The correction: U is the midpoint of T L. Nothing shows it's the midpoint of H Q.
