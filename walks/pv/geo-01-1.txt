@@ g11Term 1023
title: Geometric terms in real life
Point: one location || A point is a single location, like a star in the sky or the tip of a pencil.
Segment: straight, two endpoints || A segment is straight with two endpoints, like the edge of a ruler.
Ray: one endpoint, goes on in one direction || A ray starts at one point and goes on forever in one direction, like a flashlight beam.
Line: goes on in both directions || A line goes on forever in both directions, like a straight track that never ends.
Plane: a flat surface that goes on forever || A plane is a flat surface, like a floor or a wall that goes on and on.
the edge of a ruler: straight, with two ends → segment || The edge of a ruler is straight and has two ends, so it models a segment.

@@ g11Intersect 1002
title: What is the intersection?
two lines that cross → a point || Two different lines that cross meet at one point.
two planes that cross → a line || Two different planes that cross meet in a line, like two walls meeting in a corner.
a line crossing a plane → a point || A line that passes through a plane meets it at one point.
a line in a plane → the whole line || If the line lies in the plane, every point of the line is in the plane, so the intersection is the whole line.
parallel planes → no intersection || Parallel planes never meet.
crosses the plane, not in it → a point || In this question, the line crosses the plane but is not in it, so the answer is a point.

@@ g11Challenge 1000
title: Challenge: counting segments and rays
11 points on one line || There are 11 points on a line. Count segments first, then rays.
each point pairs with 10 others: 11 · 10 || Every segment uses two of the points. Each point pairs with the 10 others: 11 times 10 is 110.
110 ÷ 2 = 55 segments || That counts every segment twice, once from each end. So divide by 2: 55 segments.

55 segments; 22 rays || So there are 55 segments and 22 rays.
