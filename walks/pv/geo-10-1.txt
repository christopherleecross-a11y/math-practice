title: Parts of a circle
⊙D: named by its center, D || A circle is named by its center. This is circle D.
radius D̅E̅;  diameter E̅H̅ (a chord through the center) || A radius goes from the center to the circle, like D E. A diameter is a chord through the center, like E H.
chord P̅L̅: joins two points on the circle || A chord joins two points on the circle, like P L. P L doesn't go through the center, so it's not a diameter.
secant line QT: crosses the circle at two points || A secant is a line that crosses the circle at two points, like line Q T.
tangent line UX: touches at exactly one point, U || A tangent is a line that touches the circle at exactly one point, like line U X. U is the point of tangency.
a tangent: line UX || So the tangent is line U X.

@@ g101CommonTan 1000
title: Common tangents
separate circles: 4;  touching outside: 3;  crossing: 2 || A common tangent touches both circles. Two separate circles have 4. Two circles touching from outside have 3. Two circles that cross have 2.
touching inside: 1;  one inside the other: 0 || One circle inside the other and touching at one point: 1. One completely inside the other without touching: 0.
inside and touching at one point → 1 || Here the small circle is inside the big one and touches it at one point, so there's just 1 common tangent, at that point.

@@ g101IsTangent 1000
pick: Yes
title: Is the segment tangent? Yes
tangent ⊥ radius → check for a right triangle || A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency. So check whether triangle H Q L is a right triangle.
HL = 8 + 9 = 17 || The hypotenuse goes from the center to L: the radius 8 plus 9, which is 17.
8² + 15² = 64 + 225 = 289;  17² = 289 || 8 squared plus 15 squared is 289, and 17 squared is 289.
equal → right angle → tangent || They're equal, so it's a right triangle, and L Q is tangent.

@@ g101Radius 1000
title: Find the radius with a tangent
tangent ⊥ radius → right △HQL || The tangent is perpendicular to the radius at Q, so triangle H Q L is a right triangle.
legs r and 15; hypotenuse r + 9 || The legs are r and 15. The hypotenuse goes from H to L: r plus 9.
r² + 15² = (r + 9)² || Pythagorean Theorem.
r² + 225 = r² + 18r + 81 || Expand the right side: r squared plus 18 r plus 81.
144 = 18r → r = 8 || The r squared terms cancel. 225 minus 81 is 144, so 18 r is 144, and r is 8.

@@ g101TwoTan 1002

title: Tangent segments from one point
tangent segments from one point are congruent || Two tangent segments from the same outside point are congruent.
4x + 12 = 3x + 20 → x = 8 || Set them equal. Subtract 3 x and 12: x is 8.

@@ g101TwoTan 1000
kind: value\(s\) of x
title: Tangent segments from one point: x squared
LQ = LU: 4x² + 4x + 4 = 4x + 148 || The tangent segments from L are congruent. Set them equal.
4x² + 4 = 148 → 4x² = 144 || Subtract 4 x from both sides; they cancel. Then subtract 4: 4 x squared is 144.
x² = 36 → x = ±6 || Divide by 4: x squared is 36. Both 6 and negative 6 work, so x is plus or minus 6.

@@ g101Challenge 1000
title: Challenge: tangent segments of a circumscribed triangle
from L: a, from X: b, from Y: c || Tangent segments from the same vertex are congruent. Call them a from L, b from X, and c from Y.
a + b = 21,  b + c = 20,  c + a = 19 || Each side is made of two tangent segments.
2(a + b + c) = 60 → a + b + c = 30 || Add all three equations: 2 times the total is 60, so the total is 30.
a = 30 − 20 = 10,  b = 30 − 19 = 11 || Subtract: a is 30 minus b plus c, which is 10. b is 30 minus c plus a, which is 11.
