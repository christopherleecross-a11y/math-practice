@@ cm81Poly 1000
title: Area between two curves
x² = 4x − 3 → x² − 4x + 3 = 0 → x = 1, 3 || Set the curves equal to find where they cross. Those are the limits.
At x = 2: 4(2) − 3 = 5 > 2² = 4 → line on top || Test a point between them to see which graph is on top.
A = ∫₁³ (4x − 3 − x²) dx || Integrate top minus bottom.
= [2x² − 3x − x³/3]₁³ = 0 − (−4/3) = 4/3 || Evaluate the antiderivative from 1 to 3.

@@ cm81Setup 1000
title: Set up an area integral
eˣ > x for every x || Decide which graph is on top over the whole interval.
Area = ∫₁² (top − bottom) dx || The area integral is top minus bottom, between the given x-values.
= ∫₁² (eˣ − x) dx || Here the top is e to the x and the bottom is x.

@@ cm81Trans 1000
title: Area when the curves cross more than once
x³ = x → x = −1, 0, 1 || Find all the intersections: the curves switch places at x = 0.
[−1, 0]: x³ on top;  [0, 1]: x on top || Check which is on top on each piece.
∫₋₁⁰ (x³ − x) dx = 1/4;  ∫₀¹ (x − x³) dx = 1/4 || Integrate top minus bottom on each piece. Each area is one fourth.
Area = 1/4 + 1/4 = 1/2 || Add the pieces. One integral of x cubed minus x over the whole interval would wrongly give zero.

@@ cm81Y 1000
title: Area integrating with respect to y
9 − y² = 0 → y = ±3 || The curve meets the y-axis, x = 0, at y = plus or minus 3.
A = ∫₋₃³ (right − left) dy = ∫₋₃³ (9 − y²) dy || With respect to y, integrate right minus left. The left boundary is x = 0.

@@ cm82Disk 1000
title: Disk method
V = π∫ₐᵇ R(x)² dx || Each slice is a disk whose radius is the height of the region.
R(x) = eˣ → V = π∫₀¹ e^(2x) dx || The radius is e to the x. Squaring gives e to the 2 x.
= π[e^(2x)/2]₀¹ = π(e² − 1)/2 || Integrate and evaluate from 0 to 1.

@@ cm82Washer 1000
title: Washer method
x² = 4 → x = ±2 || Find where the curves meet to get the limits.
R = 4 (outer),  r = x² (inner) || The outer radius goes to the farther curve, the inner radius to the nearer one.
V = π∫₋₂² (R² − r²) dx = π∫₋₂² (16 − x⁴) dx || Square each radius separately and subtract. Do not square R minus r.
= π[16x − x⁵/5]₋₂² = 256π/5 || Evaluate.

@@ cm82Line 1000

R = 4 − 0 = 4,  r = 4 − x² || The farther boundary is the x-axis, distance 4. The nearer is the curve, distance 4 minus x squared.
V = π∫₀¹ [4² − (4 − x²)²] dx || Use the washer formula: pi times the integral of R squared minus r squared.

@@ cm82YAxis 1000

@@ cm83Setup 1000
title: Shells around a vertical line
V = 2π∫ₐᵇ (radius)(height) dx || The shell method: 2 pi times radius times height.
radius = 4 − x,  height = 2x − x² || The axis x = 4 is to the right, so the distance is 4 minus x. The height is the curve.
2x − x² = 0 → x = 0, 2 || The region runs from x = 0 to x = 2.
V = 2π∫₀² (4 − x)(2x − x²) dx || Put it together.

@@ cm83Shell 1000
title: Shell method
V = 2π∫ₐᵇ x · f(x) dx || Around the y-axis, the radius is x and the height is f of x.
V = 2π∫₁⁴ x(1/x) dx = 2π∫₁⁴ 1 dx || Here the height is 1 over x, so the product is 1.
= 2π(4 − 1) = 6π || The volume is 6 pi.
